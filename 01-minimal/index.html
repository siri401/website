I am an M.S. student at Tsinghua University, Institute for Network Sciences and Cyberspace, working on fine-grained, low-overhead measurement systems for high-speed networks and large-scale training clusters. My work covers the full path from programmable-switch telemetry and sketch algorithms to fault monitoring deployed on a production thousand-GPU training cluster.

Observability of large-scale systems

In thousand-GPU training clusters the failures that hurt most are the ones nobody can see: a slow link, a misconfigured NIC, a rank that finishes late every iteration. I work on making these systems expose what is actually happening, at a granularity that today’s frameworks and hardware counters do not provide. This includes sketch-based heavy-hitter detection for high-speed and distributed streams, where the goal is high accuracy under tight memory and reporting budgets.

System performance optimisation

Fine-grained monitoring competes with the workload for the same bandwidth, switch resources and GPU cycles, so measurement has to pay for itself. I work on bounded-overhead designs, selective sampling, and online decisions about where to spend a limited budget — and on using what is measured to make the system itself faster. One example is a rate-sensitive scheme that monitors microsecond-scale burst behavior at very low cost.

Fault reproducibility and root cause

More data does not automatically mean better answers. I work on turning a detected anomaly into a reproducible, attributed root cause rather than an intermittent event nobody can explain after the fact. This includes fault diagnosis for RDMA training networks, tracking failures across the whole connection lifecycle from connection setup onwards.
